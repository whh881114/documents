## Essay
The diagram illustrates how fabric is manufactured from bamboo plants.

Overall, this is a linear, nine-stage process which begins with planting bamboo in spring and ends with the production of clothes. Broadly, the procedure involves growing and harvesting the raw material, extracting and treating its fibres, and then turning these fibres into yarn and fabric.

At the first stage, bamboo plants are planted in spring and left to grow until they are harvested in autumn. After harvesting, the bamboo stems are cut into narrow strips. These strips are then crushed to form a liquid pulp. Next, the resulting mixture passes through a filter, which separates the long bamboo fibres from the liquid.

In the following stage, the extracted fibres are softened through the addition of water and amine oxide. Once they have become sufficiently soft, they are spun to produce yarn. This yarn is subsequently woven together to make bamboo fabric. In the final stage of the process, the finished material is used to manufacture clothing products, including socks and T-shirts.
