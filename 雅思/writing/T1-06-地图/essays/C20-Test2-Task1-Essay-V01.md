## Essay
The plans illustrate how the site of Beechwood Farm has changed since 1950.

Overall, although the crop-growing areas and several original farm facilities have been retained, the site has become far more commerical and tourist-oriented. The most significant changes are the disappearance of the sheep field and the addition of a campsite, holiday accommodation, parking areas and solar panels.

In 1950, much of the northern and eastern parts of the farm consisted of a large field for sheep. To the west of this field were three adjoing areas used for growing fruit trees, soft fruits and vegetables. A network of tracks connected these areas with a barn in the east. Further south were a farmhouse and an area for chickens, while a reiver ran along the western and southern boundaries of the farm.

Today, the three cultivation areas remain in their original locations. However, the formere sheep field has been divided into several new facilities. A camping field now occupies its central section, while solar panels have been installed in the northeast. A farm shop and a parking area have also been added near the main road in the north, with another car park in the east. The original tracks have been upgraded to roads, and holiday cottages have replaced the former barn. The barn itself has been relocated farther south, whereas the farmhouse and chicken area remain largely unchanged.
